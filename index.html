Second Grade | Our World 2: Grammar Unit (Prepositions & Pronouns)

Theoretical Framework: Prepositions of Place & Subject-Pronoun Agreement

Welcome to our interactive grammar whiteboard! Today we will learn how to describe where objects are located in a room and how to talk about singular and plural items using NatGeo's Our World 2 concepts.

Grammar I

Prepositions of Place

Words that show where things are located in relation to other objects:

  • Above: Higher than something else, without touching.
  • Under: Directly below or beneath something.
  • Next to: Beside or close to something.
  • Between: In the middle of two things.
  • Behind: At the back of something.
  • In front of: Ahead or facing something.
Grammar II

Subject-Pronoun Agreement (It / They)

We use pronouns and specific question words depending on whether an object is singular or plural:

  • Singular: Use Where's... ? and answer with It's... (e.g., Where's the lamp? It's behind an armchair.)
  • Plural: Use Where are... ? and answer with They're... (e.g., Where are the boots? They're under the bed.)

Solved Examples (15 Total: Basic, Intermediate, Advanced)

🟢 Basic Level Examples (Prepositions & Simple Pronouns)
Example 1: The glue is on the desk. Where is the glue?
Solution: It's on the desk. (Singular object uses "It's" with preposition "on").
Example 2: The pencils are next to the notebook. Where are the pencils?
Solution: They're next to the notebook. (Plural object uses "They're" with preposition "next to").
Example 3: The photo is above the notebook on the wall shelf.
Solution: It's above the notebook. Position indicates higher vertical alignment without contact.
Example 4: The scissors are next to the paper.
Solution: They're next to the paper. ("Scissors" is plural in usage, requiring "They're").
Example 5: The boots are under the bunk bed. Where are the boots?
Solution: They're under the bed. Positioned directly beneath the lower bunk frame.
🟡 Intermediate Level Examples (Combining Multiple Objects & Spatial Relationships)
Example 6: The pencils are positioned between the paper and the notebook.
Solution: They're between the paper and the notebook. Requires identifying two lateral reference points.
Example 7: The armchair is placed next to the fireplace in the living room.
Solution: It's next to the fireplace. Singular pronoun "It's" matches the single armchair.
Example 8: The rug is centered in front of the fireplace.
Solution: It's in front of the fireplace. The rug faces the fireplace hearth directly.
Example 9: The shelves are mounted above the stove in the kitchen setting.
Solution: They're above the stove. "Shelves" is plural, taking "They're".
Example 10: The bookcase is situated between the stairs and the armchair.
Solution: It's between the stairs and the armchair. Single furniture piece positioned in an intermediate gap.
🔴 Advanced Level Examples (Complex Room Mapping & Question-Answer Formats)
Example 11: Question formulation for a single window located above a sofa: "Where is the window?"
Solution: It's above the sofa. Combines correct interrogative "Where is" with exact spatial preposition.
Example 12: Question formulation for multiple photo frames on shelves: "Where are the photos?"
Solution: They're on the shelves. Plural subject-verb agreement ("Where are... They're").
Example 13: Describing a lamp hidden behind an armchair: "Where is the lamp?"
Solution: It's behind an armchair. Demonstrates obscured rear positioning.
Example 14: Analyzing a room where two matching armchairs flank a central fireplace.
Solution: They're between the fireplace and the side walls / or each is next to the fireplace. Multi-object orientation analysis.
Example 15: Full dialogue synthesis: Asking and answering about boots under a bunk bed frame.
Solution: Q: "Where are the boots?" | A: "They're under the beds." Complete structural mastery of plural subject agreement.
